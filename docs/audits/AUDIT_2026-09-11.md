## 1. Blocking issue: the global rate limiter

**Severity: high. This is the single most important finding.**

`src/server.js:99`

```js
app.use(rateLimit({ windowMs: 15 * 60 * 1000, max: 200 }));
```

Three problems, in order of seriousness.

**It is mounted before `express.static` (line 174).** Every stylesheet, script,
font, logo and favicon counts against the same 200. A first-time visitor with a
cold cache spends roughly 6–10 of their budget per page view, so a browsing
session runs out in the low tens of pages. Product photography is on
`images.salsify.com` and does not count, which is the only reason this is not
already catastrophic.

**The lockout is long and site-wide.** When it trips the server answers
`429` with `Retry-After: 641` — about eleven minutes — on *every* route,
including the homepage. I tripped it twice during this audit.

**Googlebot will trip it constantly.** There are 5,311 URLs in the sitemap.
At 200 requests per 15 minutes a full crawl takes over six days of uninterrupted
crawling, and Google will instead read sustained 429s as a sick server and
throttle or drop pages. This must be fixed before the domain is pointed at
this app, or the replatform will lose the existing Shopify rankings.

Suggested shape, not yet implemented — needs your approval:

- mount the limiter **after** static, or skip it for `/css`, `/js`, `/images`
- raise `max` substantially for page routes (1,000–2,000 / 15 min is still
  ample protection against scraping)
- keep the strict limiters already on `/admin/login` (5) and auth (10) — those
  are correctly tight and should not change

## 3. Security

**Good, and better than most.** Nothing here is an active vulnerability.

Confirmed live on response headers:

| Header | Value |
|---|---|
| `strict-transport-security` | `max-age=15552000; includeSubDomains` ✓ |
| `x-content-type-options` | `nosniff` ✓ |
| `x-frame-options` | `SAMEORIGIN` ✓ |
| `referrer-policy` | `strict-origin-when-cross-origin` ✓ |
| `x-powered-by` | absent ✓ |
| `permissions-policy` | **missing** — minor |

Also verified in source: session cookie is `httpOnly`, `sameSite: lax`, and
`secure` in production; the error handler hides stack traces in production;
the 404 handler returns a proper status; `trust proxy` is set so the limiters
key on the real client IP.

Two things worth attention, neither urgent:

**The CSRF exemption for `/api/` is a loaded gun.** `src/server.js:252` skips
CSRF for anything under `/api/`, commented "API uses own auth". `src/routes/api.js`
is nine lines long and contains a single unauthenticated health endpoint — there
is no such auth. Today that is harmless. The moment someone adds a
session-authenticated route under `/api/`, it silently inherits a CSRF bypass.
Either add the bearer-token check the comment promises, or invert the rule so
new routes are protected by default.

**CSRF token comparison is not constant-time.** `token !== expected` at
`src/server.js:258`. The token is an HMAC of the session ID, so a remote timing
attack is impractical; `crypto.timingSafeEqual` is the free fix.

**CONFIRMED: the Content-Security-Policy never reaches the browser.** On a clean
`200` the served header is `upgrade-insecure-requests` — 25 characters — while
`src/server.js:59` builds a full policy with a per-request nonce,
`strict-dynamic`, `objectSrc 'none'`, a `connectSrc` allowlist and a `frameSrc`
restricted to YouTube. The HTML *does* contain `nonce="…"`, so the app is
generating nonces for a policy nothing enforces.

It is not your `.htaccess` — the only headers set there are `Cache-Control`.
Something between Node and the browser, almost certainly Hostinger's `hcdn`
edge, is replacing the header. Until that is resolved, none of the XSS
protection that CSP was written to provide is active. This is the one item in
this section that is a live gap rather than hygiene, and it is worth a support
ticket to Hostinger asking whether CSP can be passed through untouched.

## 4. SEO

All of these are cutover-blockers rather than live problems.

**The homepage has no `<h1>`.** `views/pages/index.ejs` emits three `<h2>` and
seven `<h3>` and no `<h1>` at all. `views/pages/page.ejs` also has none, so every
CMS page is missing one. `views/pages/inspiration-guide.ejs` has two.

**Every sitemap URL claims it was modified today.**
`src/controllers/sitemapController.js:101` writes `<lastmod>${today}</lastmod>`
for all 5,311 entries. Google learns quickly that the field is noise and stops
using it. `products.updated_at` is right there.

**`SITE_URL` defaults to the wrong hostname.** Ten files fall back to
`https://bathroomvanitiesoutlet.com` when the env var is unset — but the Shopify
store canonicalises to `https://www.bathroomvanitiesoutlet.com/`, with the `www`.
If the env var is missing at cutover, every canonical and every sitemap entry
points at the non-`www` host and fights the redirect. Set it explicitly.

**Structured data is thin.** Product pages emit `Product`/`Offer`/`Brand`, and
blog and inspiration pages emit `Article`/`BlogPosting` — all good. Missing:

- `BreadcrumbList` — not present anywhere, and it is the cheapest SERP win available
- `ItemList` on collection pages
- `LocalBusiness` — you have Atlanta, Kennesaw and Roswell locations and no local schema

**On the live Shopify store**, incidentally: the `bathroom-vanities` collection
page has no meta description at all, `/pages/about-us` has two `<h1>`s and an
8-character title, and the homepage meta description is 311 characters (roughly
double what Google will show). Those *are* costing you now.

## 5. The biggest single win: image weight

**Severity: high. Confirmed by measurement, and the fix is tested.**

The collection page ships **3.41 MB of images for twelve product cards**, on a
375px phone. One image is **3,455 KB by itself**. The card it renders into is
**152 × 210 CSS pixels**.

```
source delivered   4211 x 6309   3,455 KB
card renders at     152 x  210
needed at DPR 2     304 x  420
```

CSS is 25 KB and JS is 7 KB — the front end is lean. Essentially the entire
page weight is unresized product photography, and the collection page holds
4,604 products with more loading as you scroll.

Salsify is Cloudinary-backed and will do the resizing for free. I tested three
URL forms against a real asset:

| Form | Result |
|---|---|
| unmodified | 200 — 4211×6309, **3,455 KB** |
| `…/image/upload/w_400,f_auto,q_auto/s--SIG--/…` | **404** — breaks the signature |
| `…/image/upload/s--SIG--/w_400,f_auto,q_auto/…` | 200 — 400×599, **29 KB** ✓ |

**The transformation must go after the signature, not before.** That one change
takes a 3,455 KB image to 29 KB — around 119× — with no visible difference at
card size. Applied across the grid it should take the page from ~3.4 MB to
well under 100 KB of imagery.

Worth doing as a small helper that rewrites Salsify URLs at render time, with
`srcset` for 1× and 2×, rather than hardcoding a width per template. Not built
— needs your go-ahead.

## 6. Layout and UI

**Images ship without `width`/`height`.** This causes cumulative layout shift —
the page jumps while loading, which Google measures and penalises.

| Page | Images | Missing width+height |
|---|---|---|
| `/` | 36 | 30 |
| `/collections/bathroom-vanities` | 50 | 48 |
| `/bundle-builder` | 6 | 4 |
| `/lookbook` | 43 | 0 ✓ |

`lookbook.ejs` already does it correctly and is the pattern to copy.

**Alt text is present everywhere** — no image is missing the attribute. But 24
of 50 on the collection page and 4 of 6 on the bundle builder use `alt=""`,
which tells a screen reader to skip the image. Correct for decoration, wrong for
a product photo that is the only identifier of the item.

**Two homepage images are not lazy-loaded.** If those are the hero, that is
deliberate and correct; worth a glance to confirm.

### Measured at 375 × 812

**No horizontal overflow anywhere.** `scrollWidth` equals `clientWidth` on the
homepage, the collection page and the bundle builder. The elements that sit
outside the viewport are the closed `.cart-drawer` panel at `right: 702px`,
which is where a slide-in drawer is supposed to be. I flagged it as an offender
on the first pass and it is a false positive — noting that so nobody "fixes" it.

**Tap targets are the real mobile problem.** Counting only actual controls that
are on screen, 208 of 234 are under 44px. Grouped by what they are:

| Control | Count | Rendered size |
|---|---|---|
| `.model-card-size-btn` | 122 | **20 × 22** |
| `.model-card-swatch` | 56 | **18 × 18** |
| `.heart-btn` | 24 | 34 × 34 |
| `.nav-icon-btn` | 4 | 28 × 28 |
| `.sort-select` | 1 | 33 high |

The size chips and colour swatches are the ones that matter — they are the
primary way a shopper narrows 4,604 products, and at 18–22px they are below
every touch guideline going (WCAG 2.5.5 asks 44, Apple 44, Google 48). The four
header icons at 28px are the next most-used controls on the page.

**Type is small.** On the collection page, 133 on-screen elements render below
12px:

| Class | Count | Size |
|---|---|---|
| `.product-badge` | 24 | **9px** |
| `.model-card-sizes-label` | 24 | 10.88px |
| `.model-card-swatches-label` | 24 | 10.88px |
| `.product-brand` | 24 | 11.2px |
| `.btn` | 24 | 11.52px |
| `.card-qty` | 12 | 11.52px |

9px on "GREAT VALUE" and "LOW STOCK" badges is the standout — those are
merchandising messages that are working against themselves at that size.
